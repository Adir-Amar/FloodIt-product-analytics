Day 2: Event census done (37 event types). Key findings: (1) Deliberate monetization is extremely rare — 27 purchase events by 27 distinct users (no repeat buyers) and 1,912 rewarded-ad views across 354 users in 114 days; note ad_reward covers only opt-in rewarded ads, not banners/interstitials. (3) Hypothesis, unconfirmed: two game modes (campaign level_start vs dominant quickplay ~7:1). (4) post_score (242k events / 8.6k users, ~28 per user) is far too high-volume to be a deliberate social-share action — almost certainly an automatic score submission. Verify via params. (LinkedIn material: post_score hypothesis killed by a volume sanity-check — good story about verifying event definitions before theorizing.)

Day 3: Cracked event_params via UNNEST. Findings: (1) params are not guaranteed per event — always count, never assume (e.g. time present on only 60% of post_score). (2) score is logged as int on some events, double on others (likely platform split) — rule: always COALESCE(int_value, double_value) or risk silently dropping a type's worth of data. (3) firebase_* params are SDK housekeeping, not game data. (4) score is a bounded integer 0–21, mass decaying from 0, anomalous spike at the ceiling (21) — open anomaly, unexplained. (5) Verdict: post_score = automatic score submission around level completion (volume ≈ completions, every event carries level+score); social-share theory conclusively dead. Exact trigger unresolved and deliberately not pursued.

Day 4 — Retention metric definitions (locked): Calendar-day convention via event_date (GA4 standard). Day 0 = user's earliest first_open date (MIN per user — 3 users have duplicate first_opens; cohort = 4,319 distinct users, not 4,322). Retained-on-N = ≥1 event of any type on exactly day 0 + N (caveat: includes app_remove etc., accepted noise). Eligibility anchored to dates: day 0 ≤ 2018-10-03 minus N days. Retention rate = eligible-and-retained / eligible, per N. (Meta-lesson logged: unpinned indexing conventions produced a fake disagreement between me and Claude — pin conventions in writing, always.)

Day 17 — Repo polish (notebook narrative, six corrections, score-ceiling collapse)
Session type: writing and verification, no new extraction. Two BigQuery-free passes over the five notebooks — Phase 1 code/charts/order, Phase 2 markdown/comments. Six claims carried from Days 11–16 were found wrong or unsupported and corrected; one of them was the project's headline finding. Corrections are recorded here rather than back-edited into Days 11–16: the ledger is append-only, and a record that shows the errors being caught is worth more than one where they never happened.
Correction 1 — firebase_campaign, 4.7× is a share ratio and share ratios are not rate ratios. Day 16 reported firebase_campaign elevated ~4.7× on spike days, read off the composition table (spike share 3.03e-04, interleaved 6.5e-05). But shares are compositional and screen_view had just vacated ~35.6pp of the share space (41.71% → 6.13%), so every other event's share is inflated before any behaviour changes: non-screen_view share is 0.5829 interleaved against 0.9387 spike, a mechanical inflation factor of 1.61. Fix was not to divide it out — that answers "campaign's share among non-screen_view events", which is not the question — but to abandon shares and count raw. Result: 102 firebase_campaign events across the 7 spike days against 20 across the 7 interleaved days = 5.1× on matched windows; 14.57/day against the stable window's 3.66/day (344 events / 94 days) = 4.0×. Raw counts on matched windows are immune to composition entirely. The finding survives and is stronger than the number originally claimed. Lesson: a share ratio and a rate ratio are different quantities and are not convertible without denominators; when both are available, quote the rate.
Correction 2 — notification_foreground and both dynamic_link events are too small to carry any claim. Day 16 logged "notification_foreground completely absent on spike days → weakens the push-notification theory". Counted raw, the event fires once in the entire window — 1 event, 1 user, in the 94-day anchor block of ~3.56M pre-window events — and is absent from interleaved days too. Its absence on spike days is therefore uninformative about push notifications: the app essentially never logs it. Reclassified from weak evidence to untestable. Same treatment for dynamic_link_first_open (3 events total, spike-only) and dynamic_link_app_open (3 anchor, 2 spike): the spike-only pattern is real and worthless at that count. Both dropped from findings, recorded in the notebook's "not concluded" section so their absence is deliberate rather than an oversight. Lesson: check the count behind a proportion before the proportion becomes a claim; "absent on spike days" and "absent everywhere" look identical in a share table.
Correction 3 — the composition verdict was overreaching, and reach/intensity is the discriminator that actually works. Day 16 concluded "composition shifted massively → real returning users, not a data artifact". The reasoning does not hold: a composition shift driven almost entirely by one event's collapse is exactly what a logging change for that event produces. Worse, window_group is assigned by whether a user ever logs first_open, and installs flatlined on 5 of 7 spike days — if an install wave went unlogged, those users land in pre_window by construction, so "pre-window users behaved differently" is also what the artifact hypothesis predicts. Day 14 had specced the test that separates these (per-active-user event rates by group); Day 15 recorded it as not run; Day 16 reached a verdict without it. Ran it: two quantities, reach (user-days on which an event fired, = n_users summed across dates for a single event) and intensity (n_events / user_days, conditional on participation). A logging fault cuts intensity while leaving reach intact; a real influx raises reach across independent events while intensity holds flat. Result: reach rose on level_start_quickplay 2,044 → 4,165 (2.04×), user_engagement 2,499 → 5,055 (2.02×), post_score 1,792 → 2,440 (1.36×), screen_view 2,459 → 7,854 (3.19×), with intensity across those between 0.93× and 1.13×. Twice the people, each playing about as much. Verdict "real traffic" now rests on reach growth across independent events, which is a far harder claim than composition ever supported.
Correction 4 — app-version taxonomy contamination is demonstrable, not a hedge. Carried since Day 16 as a caveat ("old app versions may log different event taxonomies, capping confidence at medium"). It is visible in the reach table. level_complete_quickplay reach fell to 274 user-days from 1,584 while post_score reach rose to 2,440 from 1,792. These two events track each other on normal days — completions accounted for 1,584/1,792 = 88% of the user-days that posted a score — and on spike days that falls to 274/2,440 = 11%. Since post_score fires around level completion (established Day 3), the completions were happening and were not being recorded. select_content reach at 0.14× and screen_view intensity at 0.05× most plausibly belong to the same effect. Consequence: the composition table cannot be read as behaviour at all, which is why the verdict was rebuilt on reach. Reclassified from caveat to finding. The install blackout on 5 of 7 spike days remains unexplained and is the strongest surviving argument for a logging fault; logged as open.
Correction 5 — there is no game-wide score ceiling at 21. This was the headline of Day 16 and it is wrong. The claim ("level mode has a hard ceiling at score 21, 3,415 posts, 28× score 20's 121") came from the pooled level-mode distribution, which sums thirty levels. Row count raised the doubt: post_score_distribution.csv is 312 rows, 16 of them quickplay, leaving 296 across 30 levels — roughly ten distinct scores per level, where a uniform 0–21 range would allow up to 660. Levels therefore do not share a score range and the pooled series is a mixture of thirty different supports. Checked per level: score 21 occurs on level 1 and nowhere else, holding 3,415 of level 1's 3,947 posts (86.5%). No other level's maximum exceeds 18. Maxima are non-monotone across the ladder (21, 18, 10, 14, 12, 12, 5, 10, 4, 10, 17, 14, 12, 13, 10, 10, 8, 6, 7, 7, 16, 13, 12, 11, 8, 7, 3, 7, 4, 5). Second check, whether posts concentrate at each level's own maximum: level 1 at 86.5%, every other level between 0.0% and 3.8% (level 13 highest), median ~0.4% — so this is not a general pile-at-the-top pattern, it is one level unlike the other twenty-nine. Corrected finding: score 21 is a level-1 phenomenon. The chart was rebuilt to plot level 1 separately from levels 2–30, because the pooled version renders a bar that a reader would take as game-wide. Lesson: before pooling groups into one distribution, check they share a support; the row count is the cheapest tell.
Consequence of correction 5 — the Day 3 open anomaly is located. Day 3 logged "score is a bounded integer 0–21, mass decaying from 0, anomalous spike at the ceiling (21) — open anomaly, unexplained". The spike is entirely level 1. Cause still unknown and not pursued: a scoring rule, a tutorial behaviour, or a first-level logging quirk would all leave this footprint, and telemetry records outcomes rather than rules. Naming a mechanic would repeat the Day 16 fabrication error.
Consequence of correction 5 — the scores 10–12 bump is still open, and one hypothesis is dead. Level-mode counts dip to 1,195 at score 10, rise to 1,669 and 1,719, then collapse to 617 at score 13. Candidate explanation was that levels whose maximum falls at 10–12 pile up there and stack. Ten levels do max in that range (3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, 15, 16, 23, 24) but contribute 70 posts between them at their maxima, against a bump of roughly 3,400. Hypothesis rejected on arithmetic. Unexplained.
Correction 6 — "17-level plateau" is a slope, not a plateau. Day 16 described levels 10–26 as a plateau at ~600–1,050 posts each. Plotted, the region declines monotonically from 977 at level 10 to 612 at level 26, a 37% loss. It read flat only because a printed Series hides a slow slide.
